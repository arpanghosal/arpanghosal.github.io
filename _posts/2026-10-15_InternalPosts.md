## Brief gist of the chapters:
**Chapter One: Observing the Armies on the Battlefield of Kurukṣetra**
Description of battlefield, armies etc. Arjuna's paralysis.
1.29 My whole body is trembling, my hair is standing on end, my bow Gāṇḍīva is slipping from my hand, and my skin is burning.

**Chapter Two: Contents of the Gītā Summarized**
Samkhya yoga -
Soul's incessant existence; souls have multiple effects on levels of understanding; Prompt action needed 2.35-40ish; Stoicism in action prescribed 60ish onward - overrides Vedic jargons and advocates for fixed mind.
2.47 You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action.

**Chapter Three: Karma-yoga**
3.3 I have already explained that there are two classes of men who try to realize the self. Some are inclined to understand it by empirical, philosophical speculation, and others by karma.
Work is incessant, actions cannot be avoided; so better use them as such; various actions lead to various outcomes. Vedic concept of sacrifice, here in purview of karma, explained.
Even God is not exempt, out of exemplary actions, 3.20ish:
3.22 O son of Pṛthā, there is no work prescribed for Me within all the three planetary systems. Nor am I in want of anything, nor have I a need to obtain anything – and yet I am engaged in prescribed duties.
Solution - Offering up actions and outcomes to God:
3.30 Therefore, O Arjuna, surrendering all your works unto Me, with full knowledge of Me, without desires for profit, with no claims to proprietorship, and free from lethargy, fight.
Material modes of nature alluded to - need to curtail 'lust' and 'gluttony'.
